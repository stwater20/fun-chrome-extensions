Why Cats Run the Internet

For decades, researchers have tried to explain why cat videos dominate the web. The answer, according to a new study from the Institute of Feline Studies, has less to do with cuteness and more to do with the way our brains process unpredictable motion.

Lead author Dr. Marisol Okafor spent three years watching people watch cats. “Every cat video is a tiny thriller,” she says. “Will it jump? Will it fall? Viewers literally lean toward the screen.”

The Science of the Pounce

The team measured attention using eye tracking. Participants watched dog videos, baby videos and cat videos. Cats won by a whisker, holding gaze for an average of 4.2 seconds longer per clip.

What This Means for Creators

If you want your content to travel, the researchers suggest building in moments of suspense. Even a recipe video can borrow from cat logic by delaying the final reveal by a few seconds.

Not everyone is convinced. Critics point out that the study only included 412 participants, most of them university students. Still, the findings match what platform data has suggested for years.
